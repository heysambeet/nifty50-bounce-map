The verdict: moving-average touches produce a mild, broad bounce — the 200 DMA is the most reliable, with 31 of 50 stocks positive. A 52-week low is the opposite: a small pop the next day that turns negative by day 2 — only 20 of 50 stocks bounce, and the losers lose big.

Method & honest caveats

  • Event definitions. DMA touch = previous close above the average and today's low at/below it. 52-week low = today's low under the prior 252-session minimum. One event per 5 sessions per stock, so a long slide isn't recounted daily.
  • Returns are close-to-close from the event day's close — what you'd get buying the close after the signal printed.
  • Small samples in the 52-week-low table. Several stocks have under 10 events (ETERNAL has one) — treat single-digit event counts as anecdotes, not edges.
  • Survivorship bias. Today's constituents applied to 10 years of history; stocks that fell out of the index (and kept falling) aren't here, which flatters the bounce numbers.
  • Tata Motors uses its post-demerger ticker (TMPV); history spans the full decade.
